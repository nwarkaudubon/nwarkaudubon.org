When landscaping, please consider how what you plant will impact wildlife, including birds. On this page we have included some information that could help you get started.

One good starting point is a summary on the Arkansas Audubon Society website. (Not to be confused with this site, which is the Northwest Arkansas Audubon Society website.) It consists of a list of shrubs, trees, grasses, and wildflowers good for Arkansas birds and butterflies, with handy source material at the end of the list.

You can find the summary here: [ar.audubon.org/go-native-birds-and-butterflies](http://ar.audubon.org/conservation/go-native-birds)

A more detailed source is a pamphlet produced by Bruce Shackleford, president of Environmental Consulting Operations, Inc.: "Go native when you go green! Making the right choices to keep the natural heritage of the Ozarks in northwest Arkansas!"

We can't encourage you enough to take a look at this work. He discusses the problems with non-native plants, why natives do better, sources for native plants, then an extensive technical list of plants native to Benton and Washington counties, plus a list of what NOT to plant, and why.
